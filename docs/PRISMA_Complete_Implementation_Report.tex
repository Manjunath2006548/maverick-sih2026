\section{Abstract and 100\% Conformance Statement}
\label{sec:abstract}

\noindent In high-reliability sectors such as space, electronic components undergo rigorous
Environmental Stress Screening (ESS), including \textbf{Burn-In testing} --- operating components at
elevated temperatures (e.g.\ \textbf{125\,$^{\circ}$C}) for extended periods. Traditional screening relies
on \emph{static parametric pass/fail limits}. However, ``latent defects'' --- components that pass the
absolute datasheet limits yet exhibit subtle, anomalous drift over time --- often escape into final
payloads and fail catastrophically in the field.

\noindent \textbf{PRISMA} is a predictive machine-learning system that analyses time-series parametric
data (standby current \keys{Iddq}, leakage currents, propagation delays) measured at the burn-in
intervals \keys{0h, 24h, 96h, 168h} to detect anomalous components before they are screened into
an operational payload. It implements both required evaluation artefacts:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Module A --- Dynamic Outlier Detection:} a multi-method statistical consensus
        (Z-Score, IQR bounds, absolute limits) that flags components against \emph{their own lot
        distribution} rather than a fixed threshold --- with explainable reasons for every verdict.
        \emph{Concrete case from the problem: a lot with an average leakage of \textbf{10\,$\mu$A}
        and a part showing \textbf{45\,$\mu$A} is a massive anomaly even though the datasheet
        maximum is \textbf{50\,$\mu$A}.}
  \item \textbf{Module B --- Time-Series Drift Predictor:} a regression model that takes
        \code{Value\_0h} and \code{Value\_24h} as inputs and forecasts \code{Value\_168h}. If the
        predicted 168h drift rate exceeds a calculated \textbf{safety slope}, the component is
        flagged for \textbf{early rejection}.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation metrics:} Anomaly Detection Score (False Negatives --- escaped defective
        parts --- penalised \textbf{10$\times$}), Drift Prediction Accuracy (MAE between predicted
        \code{Value\_168h} and hidden ground truth), and \textbf{Explainability} (every verdict
        justified to a QA inspector, never a black box).
\end{itemize}

\whatbox{Every phrase of the problem statement maps to a shipped artefact: 125\,$^{\circ}$C
  burn-in is recorded as the \code{burn\_in\_temp} column; the
  10\,$\mu$A$\rightarrow$45\,$\mu$A-in-50\,$\mu$A anomaly is caught by lot-relative consensus;
  Module B consumes exactly \code{Value\_0h} and \code{Value\_24h} to forecast
  \code{Value\_168h} and compares the drift against the safety slope; the score penalises a
  missing defect at \textbf{10$\times$}; MAE is reported per parameter; and the audit panel
  explains every classification.}

\phasebox{Phase 1}{0\% -- 15\%}{Problem Framing and Requirement Analysis}

\subsection*{Deliverable}
A complete, unambiguous reading of the problem statement into implementable requirements.

\subsection*{What was established}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Background:} aerospace components are burned in at elevated temperatures
        (125\,$^{\circ}$C) inside ESS; static pass/fail values let latent defects escape.
  \item \textbf{Description:} predict defective parts from time-series parametric data at
        0h/24h/96h/168h (\keys{iddq}, \keys{leakage}, \keys{delay}, \keys{supply\_current}).
  \item \textbf{Module A requirement:} \emph{dynamic} lot-relative outlier detection --- the

  \item \textbf{Module B requirement:} regression model from \code{Value\_0h} and
        \code{Value\_24h} to \code{Value\_168h}, with a safety-slope cutoff
        for early rejection.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation metrics:} Anomaly Detection Score (FN-catastrophic), Drift Accuracy
        (MAE vs ground truth), Explainability.
\end{itemize}

\phasebox{Phase 3}{35\% -- 60\%}{Data Ingestion and Module A (Dynamic Outlier Detection)}

\subsection*{Deliverable}
Deterministic burn-in datasets, statistical screening, and the anomaly detection score.

\subsection*{3.1 Data ingestion}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{CSV upload} --- robust hand-written parser (quoted fields, CRLF/LF, type
        coercion).
  \item \textbf{Manual entry} --- key/value measurement lines queued per component.
  \item \textbf{Sample data} --- deterministic MT19937 (Mersenne Twister, \textbf{seed 42}),
        \textbf{200 components $\times$ 5 lots}, 4 checkpoints, \textbf{20 columns}, with
        \keys{is\_defective} ground-truth labels (15 seeded defects, $\sim$8\%).
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Column set (20)} & \textbf{Purpose}\\
\midrule
\code{component\_id}, \code{lot\_id} & identity\\
\code{iddq\_0h/24h/96h/168h} & standby current at each checkpoint\\
\code{leakage\_0h/24h/96h/168h} & leakage current at each checkpoint\\
\code{delay\_0h/24h/96h/168h} & propagation delay at each checkpoint\\
\code{supply\_current\_0h/24h/96h/168h} & supply draw at each checkpoint\\
\code{burn\_in\_temp} & chamber temperature, 124--126\,$^{\circ}$C (centre 125)\\
\code{is\_defective} & ground-truth label for scoring\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The \code{burn\_in\_temp} column records the 125\,$^{\circ}$C ESS context and is \emph{excluded
from Module A screening} so environment context cannot dilute the device-risk metric.

\subsection*{3.2 Module A --- three detection methods}
For every lot and every parametric column the lot statistics (mean $\mu$, std $\sigma$, median,
quartiles $Q_{25}/Q_{75}$, min/max) are computed, then each component value is judged by:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Z-Score:} $z=(x-\mu_{\text{lot}})/\sigma_{\text{lot}}$, anomaly if $|z|>1.5$.
  \item \textbf{IQR bounds:} $[Q_{25}-1.5\cdot IQR,\;Q_{75}+1.5\cdot IQR]$, anomaly if outside.
  \item \textbf{Absolute datasheet limit} --- hard pass/fail (permissive until configured).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{3.3 Consensus classification}
$\text{votes}\ge 2$ (majority of 3) $\Rightarrow$ parameter anomaly. Component risk $=$ mean of
per-parameter risks. Final verdict: $\text{risk}>0.7$ or $>50\%$ parameters anomalous $\Rightarrow$
\keys{REJECT}; $\text{risk}>0.4$ or any anomaly $\Rightarrow$ \keys{REVIEW}; else \keys{PASS}.

\subsection*{3.4 The 10\,$\mu$A $\rightarrow$ 45\,$\mu$A $\rightarrow$ 50\,$\mu$A case}
A part at 45\,$\mu$A in a lot whose mean is 10\,$\mu$A sits visually inside the 50\,$\mu$A datasheet
wall. Static screening ships it. \textbf{PRISMA} does not --- its Z-score and IQR tests are computed
against the lot mean of 10\,$\mu$A, so 45\,$\mu$A is several sigma away and is flagged anomalous.
This is the core of the ``dynamic'' requirement and it is demonstrated live in the demo.

\subsection*{3.5 Detection score (FN penalised 10$\times$)}
\begin{equation*}
\text{Score}=\max\left(0,\;\frac{TP+TN-10\cdot FN-1\cdot FP}{N}\right)\times100\%
\end{equation*}
Defective parts flagged REJECT/REVIEW are TP; defective parts classified PASS are \textbf{escaped
defects (FN)} and cost \textbf{10$\times$}. Score 79\%, recall 100\%, zero escaped defects.

% ============================================================
\phasebox{Phase 4}{60\% -- 80\%}{Module B and Evaluation Metrics}

\subsection*{Deliverable}
From-scratch drift regressors, the safety slope, early rejection, and per-parameter accuracy.

\subsection*{4.1 Feature engineering}
From the raw checkpoints, per parameter: \code{value\_0h}, \code{value\_24h}, the drift
$x_{24h}-x_{0h}$, the drift rate $(x_{24h}-x_{0h})/24$, the percentage change
$100\cdot\Delta/|x_{0h}|$, and (when 96h exists) the 96h group plus the acceleration term.
The regression target is exactly \code{Value\_168h}. Features are standardised before training.

\subsection*{4.2 Three candidate regressors (from scratch)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Ridge regression} --- closed form, L2 ($\alpha=1.0$), hand-written matrix inversion.
  \item \textbf{Random Forest} --- 100 CART trees, max depth 10, bootstrap, mean ensemble.
  \item \textbf{Gradient Boosting} --- 100 shallow trees (depth 5, lr 0.1) fit to residuals.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{4.3 Prediction and early rejection}
Given \code{Value\_0h} and \code{Value\_24h} (optionally \code{Value\_96h}):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Build and standardise the feature vector.
  \item Predict $\hat{x}_{168h}$ with the selected model (best of 5-fold CV MAE).
  \item Predicted drift rate $r=(\hat{x}_{168h}-x_{0h})/168$.
  \item If $|r|>$ safety slope $\Rightarrow$ \keys{REJECT}; else \keys{PASS} with confidence $1-|r|/s$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{4.4 Safety slope}
\begin{equation*}
\text{safety\_slope} =
\begin{cases}
|\overline{rate}_{0\rightarrow24}|+1.96\cdot s_{rate}, & n\ge 5\\
2\times|\overline{rate}_{0\rightarrow24}|, & n<5
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
i.e.\ the lot's mean 0h$\rightarrow$24h drift rate plus a 97.5\% normal bound ($\Phi^{-1}(0.975)=
1.959963984540054$). A 6-step reasoning chain walks the inspector from raw values to the verdict.

\subsection*{4.5 Drift Prediction Accuracy (MAE vs ground truth)}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Chosen model} & \textbf{MAE} & \textbf{RMSE} & $R^2$\\
\midrule
iddq & Random Forest & 0.0718 mA & 0.1482 & 0.9883\\
leakage & Random Forest & 0.0417 mA & 0.0952 & 0.9934\\
delay & Gradient Boosting & 0.0007 ns & 0.0010 & 0.9999\\
supply\_current & Random Forest & 0.1406 mA & 0.3036 & 0.9836\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\textbf{MAE} is the metric named in the evaluation criteria: the mean absolute error between the
predicted \code{Value\_168h} and the hidden ground truth. Batch result (seed 42): \textbf{800}
predictions $\rightarrow$ \textbf{771 PASS / 29 REJECT}.

\subsection*{4.6 Feature importances}
Tree models report aggregated MSE-improvement importances (normalised to sum 1); ridge reports
$|\beta_i|$ magnitudes --- powering the ``safety analysis'' cards.

\subsection*{Verified by}
E2E checks: 4 parameters trained, 800 predictions, single iddq prediction \code{Value\_168h =
11.4747, verdict PASS}, per-parameter MAE/RMSE/$R^2$.
